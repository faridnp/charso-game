# Charso — Content Bible (Production Baseline)

## World and identity

- Game title: چهارسو (Charso).
- Fictional hub city: مهرگان (Mehrgan).
- Genre: Persian word puzzle + heritage restoration adventure.
- The city is a fictional game-world framework. It must not imply that monuments from different Iranian cities or eras historically existed together.
- The player is a contemporary apprentice entrusted with documenting and helping restore neglected heritage spaces through a fictional civic initiative. The story must not claim the player is performing real conservation.

## Core session

1. The player enters a clearly framed neighborhood objective.
2. A short character/story beat explains why the next word challenge matters.
3. The player solves a curated Persian puzzle.
4. A clear reward animation and ledger show what was earned and why.
5. The player chooses a restoration action with a visible cost and visual outcome.
6. The environment visibly changes; the next story beat or objective becomes available.
7. The player can leave at any time; the game saves safely and resumes at a sensible point.

The player should always know: What am I doing? Why am I doing it? What do I get? What changes when I finish?

## First vertical slice content target

The first vertical slice is one coherent, polished neighborhood story, not a city full of empty buttons:
- One tutorial arc.
- 15–25 editorially reviewed puzzle levels for the first polished vertical slice. The current branch now includes 220 draft Persian word puzzles (20 opening + 200 extended); structural checks cover unique IDs/answers, clue presence, metadata and exact letter inventory. All 200 new clues still require native Persian editorial review for ambiguity, spelling, tone, difficulty and accepted alternatives before release.
- One landmark with a verified research dossier and licensed/original visual reference pack.
- 3–5 restoration stages with distinct, visible environment changes.
- 2–3 original supporting characters with reviewed Persian dialogue.
- One small hub/map view with meaningful navigation.
- A complete settings, pause, help, and save/recovery flow.

The exact landmark must be selected only after source and asset-rights review. Hafezieh is a candidate, not an approved production asset. Do not create its 3D model from memory or from a random search image.

## Word-content editorial policy

Every puzzle record must define:
- Stable content ID and content revision.
- Prompt/clue, intended answer, all accepted answers, and normalized forms.
- Exact letter inventory and whether letters may be reused.
- Difficulty, topic, estimated solve time, and hint ladder.
- Language/editorial reviewer and approval status.
- Rejection rules for offensive, misleading, obsolete, excessively obscure, or disputed words.
- Source and license/provenance for any externally sourced lexical material.

A raw dictionary dump is not an approved level set. Avoid relying on unlicensed proprietary dictionaries. No proper noun is accepted accidentally through normalization.

## Heritage editorial policy

For each candidate:
- Verify official name, coordinates, city, region, period, function, and cultural context.
- Use institutional/academic/official sources where available.
- Record source title, publisher, URL, access date, relevant passage/page, and confidence.
- Record image/model creator, license, attribution, modification rights, and redistribution rights.
- Have historical and art reviewers approve the blockout before detailed modeling.
- Label uncertain facts; do not turn speculation into narration.

Initial candidate pool (research pending): Hafezieh, Saadieh, Arg of Karim Khan, Si-o-se-pol, Khaju Bridge, Naqsh-e Jahan ensemble, Ali Qapu, Shah Mosque, Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, Fin Garden, Tabatabaei House, Jameh Mosque of Yazd, Historic Bazaar Complex of Tabriz, Arg-e Alishah, Ganjali Khan Complex, and Golestan Palace.

## Visual direction

- Distinctive, warm, tactile, contemporary game art that respects Iranian architectural identity.
- Stylized realism is acceptable; generic fantasy-medieval assets are not.
- UI hierarchy and typography must be consistent and deliberate.
- The hub should feel alive through authored environmental details, subtle motion, lighting, ambience, and character moments—not through excessive particles or expensive effects.
- Keep the game legible on small phones and tablets; visual quality must scale gracefully.

## Narrative boundaries

- Characters and city events are fictional and must be clearly separated from documented heritage facts.
- Never fabricate quotes from historical figures.
- Never depict sacred or funerary places carelessly.
- Dialogue should sound natural in contemporary Persian, not translated English.
- Every educational fact is linked to its source record and reviewed before shipping.
