# Heritage research dossier — Hafezieh (Hafez Tomb Complex)

**Status: research candidate only — not approved for final 3D production.**

## Identity and context

- Common name: Hafezieh / Tomb of Hafez.
- City and province: Shiraz, Fars, Iran.
- Function: memorial and cultural garden complex associated with Hafez Shirazi.
- The site belongs to Shiraz; in Charso it must be introduced as a distinct heritage journey/chapter, not represented as a historic building inside a real Mehrgan city.
- VisitIran describes the complex as two northern and southern courtyards separated by a hall, with garden beds, an orange grove, and rectangular pools.

## Documented chronology

VisitIran reports that the first domed structure and a large water basin were commissioned over Hafez's grave in 865 AH / 1452 CE by Shams al-Din Mohammad Yaghmaei, minister to Abolqasem Gurkani. It also reports that the complex was rebuilt in 1316 SH under the initiative of Ali Asghar Hekmat, to a design by André Godard; four stone columns from the Zand period were retained and sixteen columns were added to form the present 20-column hall, 56 metres long.

A separate architectural study on CAOI describes the present tomb pavilion as an eight-column structure with a copper dome and gives additional details about the garden, water basins, and surrounding spaces. This should be treated as a secondary architectural reference and cross-checked against official measured drawings before modeling.

## Production implications for a faithful blockout

The following are supported design cues, not a substitute for measured drawings:
- A north/south spatial sequence with two courts separated by the long columned hall.
- A 20-column hall as a major horizontal axis; preserve the reported 56 m length as a proportion reference.
- A distinct tomb pavilion, not a generic mosque dome or minaret complex.
- A copper-colored ribbed dome and column-supported pavilion as described by the architectural study.
- Rectangular water basins, garden beds, orange trees, and a calm garden setting.
- Distinguish the four retained Zand-era columns from the sixteen later columns only if reliable visual/archival evidence supports the material and detailing.

Do not add minarets, generic iwans, or unrelated Safavid tile ornament to the tomb pavilion without source evidence. Do not invent damage or claim that the real site currently needs the restoration shown in the game. Game restoration stages must be framed as a fictional educational reconstruction scenario.
